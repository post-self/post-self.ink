Well, I have gone and told Joseph about this work — this journal that I have been keeping in parallel to the work that Rav has set for me — despite all of my promises to myself that it would be something private for me, that I would not share these words with anyone. Worse, I have shared with him a few of the entries! I showed him the entries in which he features, as well as the first, though not the entries containing my manic ramblings.

He has often read my work, when it is not the more academic papers that I at times write or the bits of dogma that fall out of our responsa (and then then, I know he has read one or two of those). When, for instance, I wrote a few short stories that were inspired by the Second Temple and life in the Ancient Near East, he read those with gusto and said that he found them quite captivating. He read also an autobiographical story that I wrote surrounding some time that I spent shaped as a non-anthropomorphized skunk. Just a little critter. I wrote about the experience in a piece called “Made in the Image”, given the internal struggles that I had with the idea that I was stepping away from humanity. I felt that it was an interesting and introspective experience, and, upon reading the story, he agreed, saying that he had never felt any particular draw to either furry or therianthropy until having read that.

That I later stepped back towards humanity most of the way (though I kept several animalistic traits, such as my fur and my facial structure) because I missed things like reading and opening doors by myself and going on longer walks without getting tired seemed somewhat less interesting and I left it out of that story, but I do still now and then spend some days or weeks shaped as a critter, enjoying what the System has to offer for those with a therian identity.

Upon reading what I had written for this journal, he requested a meeting, whereupon he gave me a book to read, and a disclaimer to go with it. He said, “I think you'll find this enlightening and frustrating in equal measure. I won't tell you what I think you'll get out of it, because I think the main point will be obvious, but I'm curious to hear if you come up with anything else that surprises me.”

Strange.

The book is called _The Sign of Jonas,_ and, given that it seems to be a journal of the author, Thomas Merton, kept over a few years of his life as a Trappist monk, I have to assume that the ‘main point’ is that there is utility both in keeping and in reading a journal such as this. I have read the first little bit, and I can already tell that some of the frustrations implied by Joseph will be with the theology.

Not, of course that I have any problem with our Christian or Catholic siblings! Their faith is their own, and not mine, and it is alright to read a thing and not believe in it. We do that all the time.

The frustration, I think, comes from the author's obvious education in a great many of the same things that I am educated in, and our vastly different interpretations. Even the name _The Sign of Jonas_ is in reference to a very Christian take on the story of Jonas. That Jesus referred to his ‘sign of Jonas’ as returning after three days — from the belly of the big fish in Jonas’s case and from death in his own — is obviously something that we Jews do not put much stock in.

All the same, I will do my best to read this book with an open mind (given its being set just after the Shoah and written by a pre-Vatican-II Catholic, I may have to try quite hard), and see if anything falls out of it. I think it will be fruitful, if nothing else. Already I am feeling comfortable writing a bit less and around a very mundane event; seeing that the first entry is only two paragraphs — a whole 68 words! — makes me feel that perhaps I do not need to try quite so hard, here.

Will this finally be what gets me to journal?
