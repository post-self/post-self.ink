I work with Rav all day to build the sukkah.

This, some years, is the work of an hour, or even less, as we rely on oneirotecture for the boards and cloth, the _s’chach_ that will be the roof. Decorations will be familiar ones drawn from exos or taken from the exchange.

Some years, it is the work of a day or more as we procure lumber and cloth, perhaps go sim-hopping to see if we can pick palm fronds for the _s’chach,_ paint the decorations onto the walls. One year, we even felled a tree and split the log into rough lumber to build the frame of the sukkah. We had intended to plane some of it down to make boards for the walls, but ran low on time.

This year, we have decided on an compromise. From the exchange, we pull 2x2” boards of pine, each four meters long. We build from these a rectangle measuring two meters by four, then attach the legs and rock it upright.

For the two narrower walls, we attach siding made of overlapping boards of cedar, fragrant and coarse, while the longer walls are made of thick cotton duck, a tightly woven canvas that turns the light of the sun buttery yellow.

Atop, we have chosen for the _s’chach_ boughs of yet more cedar. The feathery sprays of green cast a sweeter fragrance down over the room we have built, and block out the autumn sun in a patchy, speckled way.

Within, we place a picnic table, and we sit beside each other while we paint little decorations. Rav sits facing in toward the table, painting cast plaster pomegranates in chaotic swirls of orange, purple, pink, and red, and cast plaster etrogs in boxy spirals of yellow and green. I sit facing toward the back wall, where I paint on the canvas geometric dreams of barley, stalks and bushels both, that take homage from prairie architecture with, as I think of Hasher, the saturation dialed up high: the shadows purple and green and the stalks yellow and orange.

We finish in late afternoon and linger there on the bench. Brushes and paints have been waved away. The chalky scent of tempera paint mixes with the freshness of the cedar.
