What am I to do with this information?

Today, I met with a member of the congregation with whom I share very little in terms of commonalities beyond the obvious proximity. They are sociable and well-connected while I am quite happy with a small, quiet social circle. They are young and ebullient and full of energy, while I am old and tired. They are loud and outspoken and, while I may be quite outspoken on some issues — goodness knows I felt outspoken at times while working on Yom HaShichzur — I am far more self-contained.

We interact very little in shul and not at all outside of it. Juniper, however, was quite eager to speak with me when they heard about this project.

I should have known that this would lead to an upending of many of my ideas about the state of our world. I should have known that this conversation would send me into a tailspin, here, now, when I am already tender. I should have known that I would be left struggling.

I just do not know what I am to do with this information.

“Thank you for...ah, for suggesting this meeting,” I began. “I have been slowly broadening the circle of those I am interviewing, and I do not imagine that I would have thought to have...ah, to have asked you for quite some time, otherwise.”

“Oh, yeah, sure!” they said, bright and chipper, though there within their voice was an edge of intensity that I had not seen diminish at all in their thirty years since uploading. “Thanks for agreeing to it, then. I know I can get a bit loud for you, sometimes.”

I dipped my snout apologetically. “We move through the world in different ways.”

“That we do!” they said, laughing and casting themselves back into their chair — one of the simple wicker ones on the back patio of the shul — in a gangly heap of a person. Their movements, their very being felt chaotic to me. It was intriguing, in a way, for all that it was loud where I at times wished for quiet. “So, you have a bunch of questions to ask?”

“Yes,” I said, folding paws in my lap. “Well, a few, and I imagine that...ah, that the conversation will carry us from there.”

They nodded.

“Can you tell me what you were doing on the night of the Century Attack?”

“Oh, sure. I had an up-tree here for the Hanukkah Shabbat, but mostly I was on a gallery crawl with a bunch of friends. Felt like the right orientation, y’know? That my down-tree be out and my up-tree here? No clue why, always wondered whether things would’ve been different if I’d gone the other way ‘round, even if that doesn’t make sense in the grand scheme of things.”

“How do you mean?”

“Oh, my up-tree got got in the Attack.”

“I see,” I said, averting my gaze. “I am sorry.”

“Oh! Don’t be. Just part of the way the world works, y’know?” She laughed. “Just one of those things I think about some days. If I’d stuck around here and my up-tree’d gone out for the gallery crawl, would she still have gotten got? Or was it whoever was here that got got?”

“How do you feel about that?”

“Like, does that make me anxious or sad or whatever?” she asked, then shrugged. “It’s just idle musing. Doesn’t make me feel anything either way, I s’pose. Either I die or she does, and if it’s me, not like I’ll be around to worry about it.”

I worked to suppress a frown. “Do...ah, do you feel grateful, then, that it was not you?”

“Oh, I mean, sure,” she said, laughing. “I don’t _want_ to be dead. I hate that my up-tree died. I hate that so many died, so I guess yeah, I’m grateful I lived.”

I waited, hoping there was more to come.

Smirking, she continued. “I don’t feel sad often, What Right Have I. You know that.”

“Of course, yes.”

This had, a few years back, been a point of consternation between us. When you are a moody creature, there are no emotions that are off-limits, and when you hit three centuries, you take interest where you can get it. Intellectual pursuits, yes, and entertainments are not lacking, but also in emotional variety.
