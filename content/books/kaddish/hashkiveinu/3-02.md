systime 278+122 (may 2)
-----------------------

I can sense in the air some sort of change.

I do not quite know what the change is or will be. I thought perhaps that it might be a shift in my moods, a disturbance in the roiling current of potential that hovers just beneath the surface most days now, that sense of overflow, of almost being overwhelmed by what it means to be me. For years, for centuries, that sat often far too deep for me to access, to feel. It was there, possibly, maybe, but months and months would go by without me feeling it. My surface, as it were, would be placid and calm, still, or perhaps it would be turbulent and raucous in the unseen winds of my identity, but underneath was at best the calm, cool, stillness of my core identity, and at worst, an unknowable-ness that nevertheless did not concern me. Only in the days leading up to and following overflow would I sense any sort of turbulence there, some sort of sheer between two parts of me leading to vortices of identity whorling off into my sense of self.

Lately, though, that has been all the time. Ever since the Attack (or, I am coming to suspect, a little before), it has been there. Not always wild or distressing, for I have certainly also had plenty of good times, but it is always _there._ Not always in overflow, but always the potential...

And so I thought that perhaps it might be a shift in this that I could sense. I thought perhaps it was lessening, or strengthening, but the very fact that I could not tell which should have done well enough to convince me that it was not that.

Then, I suspected that it might be a shift in the seasons, which, they have been changing. It is a beautiful day. Perhaps one of the first truly beautiful days of the spring.

Here at Beth Tikvah, we do still run through the seasons, for the cyclical nature of the year is important to us as Jews in a very fundamental way. We have our harvest festivals and our festivals of lights. We have our times of planting and our times of memory. These are dictated by the moon and the sun and the sky and the earth below us, and such is the case even where, where it is all simulated moon, sun, sky, earth.

And so for some time, there were cold days and dark days, days of low clouds and fog, of a chill drizzle and the occasional snow — the very few days when I will don slippers to protect my otherwise bare pads from the elements — and then there were days when it was cool and gray, where the sun would only peek out fitfully and the wind would at times come up, and we would find ourselves sighing and hoping for sun. I read of someone saying, “My favorite season is always the next.” It is chill springs like this which make me agree.

But today! Ah, today, it was gorgeous. It is a green day, a bright day. It is a day for recognizing the turn in things, the mad dash uphill into summer.

So nice was it that I am down at the river on the edge of town, and I have stripped myself bare and folded my clothing and my kerchief around my necklace and set them on a dry boulder on the shore and have waded out into the water, nude, to slowly submerge myself three times and sing out my blessings. _Baruch atah, Adonai Eloheinu, melekh ha’olam, asher kid’shanu bit’vilah b’mayim hayim!_ Blessed are you, Divine Eternal who is in all things, who makes us holy by embracing us in the living water! _Baruch atah, Adonai Eloheinu, melekh ha’olam, shehechiyanu, v’kiy’manu, v’higianu lazman hazeh!_ Blessed are you, O Lover of the universe, who kept me alive, sustained me, brought me to this season! _Shema,Yisrael! Adonai Eloheinu, Adonai ehad!_ Hear, O Israel! HaShem is in all things, and we co-create each other!

I stand from the water, from this section of the river protected from view willows that yawn and stretch lazily over the water, creating a bower of green. I feel lighter, despite my sudden fur weighing me down. I am filled with light.

I move back to the shore and slick the excess water from my fur as best I can with my paws, even drawing my tail through my fingers to squeeze out as much as I can, and then I shake out my mane as best I can. A towel gets me to merely damp, dry enough to put on my clothes and trust the linen to breathe enough that I will dry the rest of the way on my way up the hill.

Along the bank of the river is a park. It is a comfortable space, with scattered picnic tables and a larger central gazebo that can keep perhaps a few dozen people dry during the rains that will come in the spring and autumn. There is also a sculpture park containing two intertwined works, each of which contains several distinct components. One consists of ten abstract figures standing together with their heads bowed over what appear to be books of some sort, all facing East. They are all quite melty looking, more shadows than people — figuratively in that the appear to be what is left after the light fades, literally, in that they are made of some smokey, translucent material — but in them I see a minyan gathering for prayer. I find it quite touching for the fact that none of them are looking up or clearly leading. They are all focused on their books, all clearly saying their prayers more to themselves than to each other, and yet they are doubtless in community with each other. The other sculpture is titled “Pittsburgh, 2048” and features a man and a woman with Middle Eastern features standing, facing each other, each with a paper cup of, one presumes, coffee in their hands. The man has a kippah on, and the woman a kufiyah around her neck, and they smile to each other with a touch of foreknowledge, of a wry understanding of what is to come, both oblivious to the people praying around them. The only thing that matters to each in the whole world is the other.

I walk through the minyan gathered there and brush my fingers against the man and woman’s hands. “Sabba,” I say, and, “Savta.”

As I make my way out of the park, I cross Ring Road, which, yes, rings the base of the butte upon which Beth Tikvah is built. It is down here where many of the residences are built. Several two-storey apartment buildings, a few stands of townhouses, and some lone cottages, all of which are quite colorful with early flowers. There is also, on the other side of the butte, another park which is less heavily wooded, as it opens out onto the prairie that comprises the rest of the sim. There are fields for playing out there, and a botanical garden full of flowers and pretty trees and shrubs, plus the community gardens where we grow many of the vegetables we eat, supplementing what we get off of the exchange.

I do not head around there today, though, as pretty as it is. I have a meeting this afternoon and should not dally too long. I have enough time, however, to take a leisurely stroll up the hill and enjoy the beauty of my home, and to finish drying off after my dunk in the river.

And so instead I start up the hill along the main path and stop first by the laundry. With most of our clothes — indeed, most fabric, which is templated off an initial bit of very clever oneirotecture — they will pop themselves clean if you pinch for a few seconds on a corner of the fabric. However, many cladists, myself included, do not have all of the fabric items that we might need, such as towels, in our homes, and a good number of us enjoy the physicality of pulling things off the shelf, rather than pulling it from the exchange. I do not know if this is a preference that is more common here in Beth Tikvah, which tends toward the prosaic, but the laundry is a place where one can pick up things such as towels, robes, and bedding, and then drop it off when one is done with it.

I toss my towel in the chute out front — which some newer uploads have taken to calling ‘the deke’ for reasons I do not understand — where it simply disappears. Within the laundry are shelves with many more towels, and I like to picture the number of towels on those shelves going up by one, even if such is not quite how it works.

Continuing up the path, I pass by a few more houses on one side of the path, these housing entire families. I have met all of the families that live there, whether they choose to do so in order to be within a Jewish community or because they are new uploads who are being met by families in a temporary living situation surrounded by friends.

On the other, uphill side, a few shops line the way, offering mostly food goods, but also some handmade goods and various bits of Judaica. There, too, is the coffeeshop that makes a most delightful mocha. I marvel for the thousandth time that we still call them shops and stores, despite the lack of currency.

My breathing picks up as the road zig-zags its way up the butte. It is not a very high hill but the walk has my heart rate up and the sun is starting to warm my fur.

I pass the library-_cum_\-community center, which takes up the whole of one hairpin turn in the road, offering meeting rooms, class rooms, and, of course, reading rooms with access to all the books available in the perisystem. there is where the chavruta that I share with Ravs From Whence and Sorensen meets, a room I have been hesitant to enter since the Attack.

While I could take the shortcut path nearly straight up the hill to the shul, I have enough time and it is an awful nice day out, so I continue around the corner and along the main road.

The next hairpin is occuped on the outside by the refectory. With as much time as I have, I slip inside, enjoying the cool air that greets me. There is a cafeteria with hot food set out on steam tables, a kitchen for cooking what one would like, and a wall of small doors, perhaps half a meter square, which contain a variety of ready-made dishes. Our own little automat.

I select from this bank a bowl of citrus fruits separated into their component wedges. There are oranges, tangelos, sweet limes, and grapefruit. Over top of these wedges is some chiffonaded mint and a spritz of sugar water. The perfect treat to stay cool on the rest of the climb.

Along the road to the next hairpin, I stop by my apartment, a ground-floor unit in a small complex of staff housing. Within the den/office, I pick up my satchel from where it hangs beside my desk, and from within my bedroom/library, I pick up my folio of notes, left on the bedside table from where I had been going over them this morning. I pause in the den on my way out, looking out the back door to the little patio garden that I have. A moment’s quiet before I continue. The taste of citrus on my tongue. The bitterness of grapefruit, the floral whiff coming from the tangelos, the faint shine of mint.

This is my home.

This is my life, here in Beth Tikvah.

A two-room apartment. A communal kitchen. A library. A recreation center on the other side of the butte. Hot springs up in the foothills. Friends. Love. Light.

I have decided that the change that I feel in the air is more momentous than just the shift in the seasons, or that essential restlessness of near-overflow that has been constant for the last few months. I think that I am starting to unearth something much larger within myself. I do not know quite what it is just yet. It is as though, digging through the dust and shale, I have come across a right angle. A corner of some submerged truth. There is something about me, and there is something about the world, and I will figure it out soon, but until then, it is a bunch of careful digging, gently exposing this new reality.

Something about me. Something about the world...

When I finish my citrus salad, I wave the bowl away into nonexistence, disposable object that it is, and then pad back outside. Squinting pleasantly in the sun for a few moments, I breathe deep of the fresh air — a cool air — a thick air, thicker than we were used to back in Colorado before uploading, thicker and more humid — and then I continue up to the final hairpin turn.

Here, I come across the New Upload Support Office on the outer bend, a complex that works to connect newly uploaded Jews to various communities that might interest them, and on the inner bend sits my Jonah bench. I am tempted to rest here, too, and simply enjoy the sun and the greenery of spring, but I would like to stop by Rav’s before I meet with my committee in the social hall, and so I trudge on past, taking the final bit of steepness as the road turns up towards the shul itself, growing stairs to help in the climb.

I turn aside just before reaching the top, following a path that leads around the side of the shul, down a narrow alley between that and one of the classrooms.

I press my ear to the door marked _B_ and listen for a moment. I can hear Rav within, singing quietly to herself, and no other conversation, so I knock on the door. She greets me with a smile, and we share a long, silent hug, and that, more than anything else, is all I need to carry me up the rest of the way to the shul for my meeting, and through the rest of the day.

Friends, love, and light.
